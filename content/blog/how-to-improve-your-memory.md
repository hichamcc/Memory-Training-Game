**Short answer:** the most reliable ways to improve your memory are to test yourself instead of re-reading, spread your reviews out over days, turn information into vivid images, group it into meaningful chunks, sleep well and exercise. Memory techniques like the memory palace can more than double how much you remember from a list, and anyone can learn them.

Below, each technique is explained with what it is, why it works and how to start today.

## 1. Test yourself instead of re-reading

Re-reading feels productive, but it mostly builds familiarity, not memory. Trying to *recall* information is what strengthens it. Psychologists call this the **testing effect** (or retrieval practice).

In a well-known 2006 study by Roediger and Karpicke, students who practised recalling a text remembered noticeably more a week later than students who spent the same time re-reading it, even though the re-readers felt more confident.

**How to start:** after reading something, close it and write down or say what you remember. Then check what you missed.

## 2. Space out your reviews

The German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus showed in the 1880s that we forget new information quickly at first, then more slowly: the **forgetting curve**. Each time you review something just as you're starting to forget it, the memory lasts longer.

**How to start:** review new material after one day, then three days, then a week. Flashcard apps that use spaced repetition do this scheduling for you.

## 3. Turn words into pictures

Our brains remember images far better than abstract words, especially strange or funny ones. Every technique memory athletes use is built on this. To remember "umbrella, lion, piano", picture a lion bursting out of an umbrella and smashing a piano.

**How to start:** learn the [Linking Method](/blog/linking-method-beginners-guide), the simplest way to turn a list into a chain of images.

## 4. Use a memory palace

The **memory palace** (or Method of Loci) means placing images along a route you know well, such as the rooms of your home, then walking through it in your mind to recall them.

It's the technique behind most memory competition results, and it's learnable. In a 2017 study published in *Neuron*, people with no training practised it for about 30 minutes a day for six weeks and more than doubled how many words they could remember. [Read the beginner's guide](/blog/build-your-first-memory-palace).

## 5. Chunk information

Working memory can only hold a handful of items at once: modern estimates put it at **around four chunks**. The trick is to make each chunk bigger. A phone number is easier as 555-867-5309 than as ten separate digits, and easier still if a chunk means something to you. [Learn how chunking works](/blog/what-is-chunking).

## 6. Sleep on it

A lot of memory consolidation (the process that turns fresh memories into lasting ones) happens during sleep. Studying late and then sleeping poorly works against you.

**How to start:** if something matters, review it in the evening and get a full night's sleep before you need it.

## 7. Move your body

Regular aerobic exercise is good for the parts of the brain involved in memory. In a 2011 study in *PNAS*, older adults who walked briskly for a year saw the hippocampus, a key memory area, grow by about 2%, while it shrank slightly in a stretching-only group.

**How to start:** a brisk 30-minute walk a few times a week is enough to begin.

## Which technique should you start with?

If you want quick results today, start with **turning information into images** using the [Linking Method](/tactics/linking-method). It takes a few minutes to learn. Then add **self-testing** and **spaced review** to everything you study. Once linking feels natural, move on to the memory palace.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you really improve your memory?

Yes. Memory athletes are not born with special brains: studies of top competitors found normal intelligence and brain structure. What sets them apart is the techniques they use, and those can be learned by anyone.

### How long does it take to improve your memory?

You can notice a difference within your first session with a technique like linking. Bigger gains, like those in the memory palace studies, come from short daily practice over a few weeks.

### What is the fastest way to memorize something?

For a list, use the Linking Method or a memory palace. For facts you need to keep, test yourself and review on a spaced schedule. For numbers, chunk them or learn the Major System.

### Do brain-training games improve memory?

General brain games tend to make you better at the games themselves. Practising a specific memory technique is different: it trains a strategy you can use on real information, like lists, names and numbers.
