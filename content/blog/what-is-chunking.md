**Short answer:** chunking is grouping small pieces of information into larger, meaningful units, called *chunks*, so you can hold more in your head at once. Your working memory can only juggle a few items, but each item can be big. "F-B-I-C-I-A-N-Y-P-D" is ten letters; "FBI, CIA, NYPD" is three chunks.

## Why does chunking work?

Working memory, the mental "workspace" you use to hold things for a few seconds, is small.

In 1956, psychologist George Miller published a famous paper called *The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two*, suggesting we can hold about seven items. Later research, including a widely cited 2001 review by Nelson Cowan, puts the real limit closer to **about four chunks**.

The key insight from Miller's work still stands: the limit is on the number of *chunks*, not on their size. If each chunk is a bigger unit of meaning, you can hold far more information in the same space.

## Everyday examples of chunking

- **Phone numbers:** 5558675309 is hard; 555-867-5309 is three chunks.
- **Card numbers and IBANs** are printed in groups of four for the same reason.
- **Acronyms:** NASA, DNA and UNESCO each pack several words into one unit.
- **Dates:** 149217761945 is twelve digits, or three famous years: **1492** (Columbus), **1776** (US independence), **1945** (end of WWII).
- **Music:** musicians remember chords and phrases, not individual notes.

## The expert advantage: chess

A classic 1973 study by Chase and Simon showed chess masters board positions for just a few seconds. Masters recreated real game positions far better than beginners. But when the pieces were placed randomly, the masters' advantage mostly disappeared.

Their memory wasn't generally better; they had learned thousands of meaningful patterns (chunks) from real games. This is why expertise in any field makes related information easier to remember.

## How to use chunking

1. **Split** long sequences into groups of 3 or 4.
2. **Look for meaning** in each group: a year, an age, a famous number, a pattern like 1234 or 7777.
3. **Give chunks a picture** if you can. That's where the [Major System](/blog/major-system-memorize-numbers) comes in: it turns any chunk of digits into a word.
4. **Rehearse the chunks**, not the individual items.

## Chunking vs other memory techniques

Chunking is the simplest technique on this site, and it combines well with the others. Chunking reduces *how many* things you need to remember; techniques like the Major System or a [memory palace](/blog/build-your-first-memory-palace) make each thing easier to remember.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is chunking in psychology?

Chunking is the process of grouping individual pieces of information into larger, meaningful units. It lets people work around the small capacity of working memory.

### How many chunks can working memory hold?

Miller's 1956 paper suggested about seven, plus or minus two. More recent research suggests around four chunks for most adults.

### What is an example of chunking?

Remembering the phone number 5558675309 as 555-867-5309 is chunking. So is remembering the letters FBICIANYPD as three familiar acronyms: FBI, CIA and NYPD.

### Does chunking help with studying?

Yes. Grouping related facts, building acronyms, and organising notes into a few clear categories all use chunking, and they make material easier to hold in mind and recall.
