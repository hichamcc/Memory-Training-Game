Try this. Read the list below once, then look away and say it back in order:

**umbrella, lion, piano, kettle, bicycle, iceberg, guitar, nest**

Most people get four or five. A memory athlete would get all eight without trying, and not because they were born with a better brain. They used a trick, and the simplest one is called the **Linking Method**.

## How it works

Your brain is bad at remembering abstract lists, but very good at remembering strange things that *happen*. The Linking Method turns a list into a chain of short, vivid scenes. Each scene joins one item to the next.

Here's the list above as a chain:

1. An **umbrella** opens and a **lion** falls out of it, roaring.
2. The lion sits down at a **piano** and smashes the keys with its paws.
3. The piano is boiling, and steam pours out like a giant **kettle**.
4. The kettle's spout grows wheels and turns into a **bicycle**.
5. The bicycle crashes straight into a huge **iceberg**.
6. Inside the iceberg, frozen solid, is a **guitar**.
7. The guitar's strings are woven into a bird's **nest**.

Now cover the list and run through the story. Umbrella… lion… piano… You'll find each picture pulls the next one along with it.

## The four rules that make it stick

The technique only works if the pictures are memorable. These four rules are what separate a weak link from one that lasts for days.

- **Make it absurd.** "A lion near a piano" is forgettable. "A lion *playing* the piano with its paws" isn't. Your brain filters out normal things and holds on to odd ones.
- **Add action.** Things should crash, explode, melt, grow or chase each other. Still pictures fade fast.
- **Exaggerate.** Make things enormous, tiny, or a thousand of them at once.
- **Use your senses.** Hear the lion's roar. Feel the cold of the iceberg. The more senses a scene involves, the more ways your memory has to find it later.

> Only link two items at a time. Don't try to build one big scene with the whole list in it. Each link is a small, separate picture: A meets B, then B meets C.

## Common mistakes

**Going too fast.** At first, each link takes a few seconds to build. That's normal. Speed comes with practice, and a clear image is worth more than a quick one.

**Using the same kind of scene every time.** If everything "crashes into" the next thing, the links blur together. Mix up the actions.

**Skipping the review.** After you've built the chain, run through it once from start to finish. That single pass makes a big difference.

## Where to use it

The Linking Method works best for short lists where order matters: a shopping list, the points of a speech, the steps of a recipe, or things to pack for a trip. For longer lists (twenty items or more) a single broken link can lose you everything after it. That's where the [memory palace](/blog/build-your-first-memory-palace) comes in, because it gives every item its own fixed place.

## Practice it now

Like any skill, this gets easier with practice. Start with five words and build up from there.
