**Short answer:** the peg system uses ten words that rhyme with the numbers one to ten (one-**bun**, two-**shoe**, three-**tree**…). You learn these "pegs" once. Then, to memorize a list, you hang each item on its peg with a vivid image. Because each item is tied to a number, you can recall them in any order: "what was number 7?" works instantly.

## The 10 rhyming pegs

| Number | Peg | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bun | a burger bun |
| 2 | Shoe | a sneaker |
| 3 | Tree | a big oak tree |
| 4 | Door | a front door |
| 5 | Hive | a buzzing beehive |
| 6 | Sticks | a bundle of sticks |
| 7 | Heaven | an angel on a cloud |
| 8 | Skate | an ice skate |
| 9 | Wine | a glass of wine |
| 10 | Hen | a hen |

Say them out loud a few times: "one is bun, two is shoe, three is tree". The rhymes do most of the work, and most people know all ten in a few minutes. (Some versions use "gate" for eight; "skate" is easier to picture.)

## How to use the pegs

Say you need to remember a to-do list: **call the bank, buy milk, book the dentist, water plants, send the invoice**.

1. **Bun + bank:** a burger bun is answering a ringing phone at the bank counter.
2. **Shoe + milk:** your sneaker is overflowing with milk, squelching as you walk.
3. **Tree + dentist:** a dentist is drilling into a tree that screams in pain.
4. **Door + plants:** your front door is completely covered in vines that need watering.
5. **Hive + invoice:** angry bees are swarming out of a hive made of paper invoices.

Now: what was number 3? Think *tree*, and the screaming tree with the dentist comes straight back.

## Peg system vs memory palace vs linking

| Technique | Best for | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| [Linking](/blog/linking-method-beginners-guide) | short lists in order | one broken link loses the rest |
| **Peg system** | short lists where position matters, recall in any order | only as many items as you have pegs |
| [Memory palace](/blog/build-your-first-memory-palace) | long lists, speeches | takes time to build routes |

Use pegs when you need **random access**: "what's the fourth step?" or "what's item 9?"

## Can you go beyond 10?

Yes. Popular ways to extend it:

- **Shape pegs:** pegs that look like the number: 1 = pencil, 2 = swan, 8 = snowman.
- **Major System pegs:** use the [Major System](/blog/major-system-memorize-numbers) to make a peg for every number up to 100 (11 = tot, 12 = tin, 13 = tomb…).

## Can you reuse the pegs?

Yes, but give the old images a day or two to fade before reusing the same pegs for a new list, or the two lists can blur together.

## Practice it

The [Peg System practice game](/tactics/peg-system/practice) teaches you the pegs, shows you a word for each one, and then asks for them in random order, which is exactly the skill that makes pegs useful.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the peg system in memory?

It's a mnemonic technique where you pre-learn a set of "peg" words linked to numbers, usually by rhyme, and then attach new items to those pegs with vivid images so you can recall them by number.

### What are the 10 peg words?

The classic rhyming set is: 1 bun, 2 shoe, 3 tree, 4 door, 5 hive, 6 sticks, 7 heaven, 8 skate (or gate), 9 wine (or vine), 10 hen.

### Is the peg system better than a memory palace?

Neither is better overall. Pegs are quicker to set up and let you recall items in any order; a memory palace scales much better to long lists.

### How many items can you memorize with the peg system?

With the basic rhyme set, ten at a time. Using shape pegs or Major System pegs, you can extend it to 100 or more.
