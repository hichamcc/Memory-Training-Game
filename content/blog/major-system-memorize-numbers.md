**Short answer:** the Major System turns numbers into words. Each digit 0–9 stands for a consonant sound; you fill in vowels to make a word, then picture that word. For example, 32 is M and N, which becomes **moon**, and 314159 becomes **meteor tulip**. Pictures are far easier to remember than strings of digits.

## The Major System table

Learn these ten sounds. Each one has a memory hook to make it stick.

| Digit | Sounds | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | s, z | **z**ero starts with z |
| 1 | t, d | t has one downstroke |
| 2 | n | n has two downstrokes |
| 3 | m | m has three downstrokes |
| 4 | r | fou**r** ends in r |
| 5 | l | L is the Roman numeral for 50 |
| 6 | j, sh, ch, soft g | a J looks like a backwards 6 |
| 7 | k, hard g | a K is made of two 7s |
| 8 | f, v | a cursive f looks like an 8 |
| 9 | p, b | p is a mirrored 9 |

The vowels (a, e, i, o, u) and the letters **w, h and y** have no value. They're free fillers you add to make words.

## How to turn a number into a word

1. **Split the number** into pairs or small groups: 1969 → 19 / 69, or keep it whole.
2. **Convert each digit to its sound:** 1969 → T, P, SH, P.
3. **Add vowels** to make a real word or phrase: T-P-SH-P → **top ship**.
4. **Picture it vividly:** a rocket (the top ship of all ships) blasting off: the 1969 moon landing.

## The one rule everyone gets wrong

The Major System works on **sounds, not spelling**.

- **Silent letters don't count.** "Knee" sounds like N, so it's 2, not 72.
- **Double letters count once** if they make one sound. "Butter" is B-T-R = 914.
- **Letters take the sound they make.** The "c" in "cat" sounds like K (7); in "city" it sounds like S (0).

## Worked examples

- **32** = M, N → moon, man, mine
- **41** = R, T → rat, root
- **85** = F, L → file, fly
- **94** = B, R → bear, bar
- **10** = T, S → toes, toss
- **314159** (the first digits of pi) = M-T-R / T-L-P → **meteor, tulip**: picture a meteor crashing into a giant tulip.

To remember a long number, make a word for each chunk and link the pictures into a story, or place them along a [memory palace](/blog/build-your-first-memory-palace).

## Should you prepare a word for every number?

Yes, eventually. Experienced users build a fixed list of 100 images, one for each number from 00 to 99 (00 = sauce, 01 = suit, 02 = sun…). Then any long number becomes a quick series of ready-made pictures, and you don't have to invent words on the spot. Start with 00–20 and add ten more each week.

## Major System vs PAO and Dominic

The Major System is the easiest number system to start with, because you don't need to memorize any list before using it. [PAO](/blog/pao-system-guide) and the Dominic System pack more digits into each scene, which is faster at competition level but takes much longer to set up.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Major System used for?

It's used to memorize numbers of any kind: phone numbers, PINs, dates, historical years, math constants like pi, and as a base for card memorization.

### How long does it take to learn the Major System?

Most people learn the ten digit sounds in under an hour. Becoming fluent, so you can convert numbers to words quickly, takes a few weeks of short practice.

### Do vowels count in the Major System?

No. Vowels, and the letters w, h and y, have no number value. You add them freely to turn consonant sounds into words.

### Is the Major System better than chunking?

They work well together. Chunking groups digits into smaller pieces; the Major System then turns each piece into a picture, which is much easier to remember than the digits themselves.
